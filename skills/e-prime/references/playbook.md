# playbook — Repair strategies, phrase banks, pitfalls, drills

## How to repair, function by function

### Identity (`X is Y`)

The claim hides a speaker and freezes a process. Repair by naming the namer,
dating the role, or citing the observation:

```text
The cat is Garfield.          → People call the cat "Garfield".
John is a farmer.              → John farms three acres.
                                → John had a job as a farmer, last I knew.
The electron is a particle.    → Under these instruments, the electron behaves as a particle.
Clapton is God.                → Eric's fans say he has some of the characteristics of God.
Joe is a pig.                  → Mrs. Bloggs insists Joe behaves like a pig at mealtimes.
John is a thief.               → The court convicted John of theft. / I saw John steal.
The CEO is a leader.           → The board promoted her after the turnaround she ran.
```

### Class membership / inclusion

```text
Garfield is a cat.            → Garfield belongs to the cat species.
A cat is an animal.           → "Cat" denotes an animal. / Zoologists classify cats as animals.
Mice and voles are rodents.    → Zoologists group mice and voles under rodents.
```

### Predication (`X is ADJ`)

Attribute the evaluation to its owner, or replace it with the observation
that produced it:

```text
The cat is furry.              → The cat feels furry. / The cat has fur.
The rose is red.               → I classify the rose as red. / The rose looks red to me.
The radiator is hot.           → The radiator feels hot. / The thermometer reads 80°C.
Joe is stupid.                 → In my view, Joe behaves stupidly.
                                → Joe's test scores fell below the class median.
The plan is weak.              → The plan looks weak to me: it names no owner for the rollout.
I am a failure.                → I failed the exam; I can retake it.
                                (Albert Ellis's pattern: rate the deed, never freeze the doer.)
Sarah is a dental hygienist.   → Sarah cleans teeth from nine to five.
Your dress is awful.           → I don't like your dress.
You are wrong.                 → I don't see it that way.
```

### Progressive (`is -ing`)

```text
The dog is chasing the cat.    → At present, the dog chases the cat.
I am writing a report.         → I write a report. / I sit writing a report.
It is raining.                 → Rain falls. / I hear rain on the roof.
He is being difficult.         → He acts difficult right now.
                                (Note: the `being` vanishes with the rest.)
```

### Passive

Restore the agent; when nobody knows the agent, say so; when the agent
matters, say that too:

```text
Mistakes were made.            → Cindy made mistakes. / Someone erred; we haven't found who yet.
The window was broken.         → Somebody broke the window. / The window broke.
The data were not available.   → Contractor personnel never switched on the antenna.
                                (Bourland's satellite-report story.)
The results were surprising.   → The results surprised us.
She was seen leaving.          → The doorman saw her leave.
The bill was passed.           → The Senate passed the bill.
The work got done.             → The work got done. ("got" — scruffy but honest, per Bourland.)
```

### Existential

```text
There is a problem.            → A problem exists. / I see a problem. / Problems arise here.
There are three options.       → Three options present themselves. / We see three options.
There is no solution.          → We have not yet found a solution.
```

### Locative

```text
The book is on the shelf.      → The book rests on the shelf. / The shelf holds the book.
The meeting is at 3.           → The meeting starts at 3. / We meet at 3.
The cat is here.               → I hear the cat at the door.
John is in Denver.             → John lives in Denver these days. / John flew to Denver Monday.
```

### Ownership

```text
The cat is theirs.             → They own the cat. / The cat belongs to them.
The fault is mine.             → I caused the fault; I'll fix it.
```

### Comparison, measure, date, age

```text
X is taller than Y.             → X stands taller than Y — by two inches, per the tape.
Today is Tuesday.              → Today falls on Tuesday.
She is thirty.                 → She has lived thirty years.
Two plus two is four.          → Two plus two equals four.
The table is four feet long.   → The table measures four feet.
```

### Idioms & set phrases

```text
so be it                       → Very well then.
be that as it may              → Granted, and still.
let it be                      → leave it alone
to be continued                → Continues next issue.
be right back                  → back shortly
how are you?                   → How goes it?
who's there?                   → Who stands there? / Who goes there?
where were we?                 → Where did we leave off?
it's been ages                 → Years passed since we met.
what's up?                     → What news? (Beware: "What's happening?" smuggles in a banned
                                  contraction through the reflex.)
```

### Birth

Even veterans fight over `was born`. Options: `My mother bore me in 1990`,
`I arrived in 1990`, `1990 saw my birth`. Pick whichever fits the register;
in casual speech, most E-Primers let this one slide (soft mode).

## Phrase banks

Rotate patterns; every bank, overused, breeds its own creep.

**Attribution** (restores the deleted speaker):

```text
in my view · to me · per my read · as of Friday · from where I sit ·
it seems to me · my measurements show · X maintains that · according to X ·
I rate · I classify … as · I recall … as
```

**Similitude & behavior** (repairs identity):

```text
behaves like · resembles · functions as · passes for · correlates with ·
has the attributes of · takes after · matches · models · approximates
```

**Continuity** (repairs progressive):

```text
continues to · keeps on · carries on · persists · sits …-ing · now
```

**Honest passives** (when the agent truly stays unknown):

```text
someone … (we haven't found who) · got … (scruffy but honest) ·
the … broke / fell / opened (middle voice)
```

## Quality pitfalls

### The euphemism swap

`is` → `exists as` / `qualifies as` merely re-spells identity (Ralph
Kenyon's "abuse" of E-Prime). Rewrite the claim instead.

### I-creep

Every sentence starting with `I find / I think / I see` turns the writer
into an ego parade. Alternate among "to me", "per my read", "as of Friday",
and plain verbs that carry the observer implicitly: `The film bored me`.

### Noun-creep

Bad E-Prime bloats: `the exhaustiveness of the coverage adequacy of the
Times`. De-nominalize: `The Times claims to cover everything; I doubt it.`

### Smuggled identity

- **Attributive adjectives**: `the squalid capitalists` judges inside a noun
  phrase, with the copula nowhere in sight.
- **"as"**: `I see him as a threat` still freezes the label — prefer
  `he threatens me when he …`.
- **"became" / "find"**: `He became a bore`, `I find her lazy` reassert
  essence. Keep the behavior, drop the essence: `He bores me lately`;
  `Her last three reports arrived late`.

### The Kenyon ladder

`I found the movie more rewarding than the novel` still treats reward as a
property of objects. Climb down: `I evaluated the movie as more rewarding
than the novel` — and land on the lowest rung: `I liked the movie better
than I liked the novel`. Shortest distance to the observer wins.

### Stiffness test

Read the rewrite aloud. `A problem exists` sounds like a robot in dialogue;
`We have a problem` or `Trouble!` keeps the register human. Bourland
demanded vigorous clarity — when a rewrite turns stiff, the repair went
wrong, not the rule.

### When to decline a rewrite

- Formal logic and definitions — allow `equals` and named definitions.
- Quoted speech in fiction, dialect, lyrics — characters speak like
  people, not like semanticists. Elvis keeps
  `You ain't nothin' but a hound dog`.
- Titles, names, citations — the standard carve-outs.
- Calibration-heavy or formal-logic deliverables — warn the user first
  (see `references/llm-notes.md`): the research shows degradation on some
  models for syllogism and calibration tasks.

## Drill bank (tutor mode)

### Level 1 — identity & predication

```text
1. The electron is a particle.
2. Grass is green.
3. John is a farmer.
4. Sarah is a dental hygienist.
5. The rose is red.
6. Beethoven is better than Mozart.
```

### Level 2 — passive, progressive, existential, locative

```text
1. Mistakes were made.
2. I am writing a report.
3. There are three options.
4. The data were not available.
5. He is being difficult.
6. The meeting is at 3.
```

### Level 3 — traps

```text
1. He's gone, and John's book is gone too.   (mixed readings of 's)
2. Let's be careful.                          (imperative after an exempt contraction)
3. I was born in 1990.                        (fixed-phrase passive)
4. Two plus two is four.                      (math)
5. So be it.                                  (mandative)
6. It's been ages.                            (double contraction risk)
```

### Answer key (model answers)

```text
L1.1  Under these instruments, the electron behaves as a particle.
L1.2  Grass registers as green to most human eyes.
L1.3  John farms three acres.
L1.4  Sarah cleans teeth from nine to five.
L1.5  I classify the rose as red.
L1.6  In my present mixed state of musical education and ignorance,
      Beethoven seems greater than Mozart to me. (Wilson's phrasing)

L2.1  Cindy made mistakes. / Someone erred; we haven't found who yet.
L2.2  I write a report.
L2.3  Three options present themselves.
L2.4  Contractor personnel never switched on the antenna.
L2.5  He acts difficult right now.
L2.6  We meet at 3.

L3.1  He left, and John's book vanished too.
L3.2  Let's take care.
L3.3  My mother bore me in 1990.
L3.4  Two plus two equals four.
L3.5  Very well then.
L3.6  Years passed since we last met.
```

Grading rubric: token compliance (linter clean) plus spirit — euphemism
swaps and smuggled identity fail even when the tokens pass.
